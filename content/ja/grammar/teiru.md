ている is the て form of a verb followed by いる, "to be there". It presents what the verb names as something that holds at the time spoken of, rather than as an event that happens. Its uses differ in what it is that holds.

ている conjugates as いる does: 読んでいない, not; 読んでいた, past; 読んでいます, polite. In casual speech いる often loses its い: 読んでる. Writing and formal speech keep the full form.

The verbs of existence, ある and いる, do not take ている.

## Notes

1. Kansai speech also uses いてる, which standard Japanese does not.
