A change has happened, and its result still holds.

私の車は壊れている。 My car is broken.

## How it is built

As for an action in progress: the て form, then いる. 壊れる → 壊れて → 壊れている. In speech: 壊れてる.

## Which verbs

Verbs of a change that is over in a moment: 壊れる, break; 起きる, wake up; 来る, come; 行く, go; 持つ, take hold of; 結婚する, marry; 死ぬ, die; 知る, come to know. With these, ている describes the state after the change.

Several of them come out in English as a verb of state, not as "-ing":

- 持っている, has (has taken hold of)
- 知っている, knows (has come to know)
- 起きている, is awake (has woken up)
- 行っている, is away there (has gone)

## Look-alikes

**An action in progress** (known). The form is the same; the verb decides. If the action can go on for a while, as 読む, 待つ and 見る do, ている means it is going on. If it is a change that is over in a moment, ている means its result.

**Not yet.** With まだ and a negative, the change has not happened: まだ来ていない, (he) hasn't come yet. The plain past 来なかった says only that he did not come on some occasion.

**てある** (later). Also a resulting state, but of something someone did on purpose: 窓が開いている, the window is open; 窓が開けてある, the window has been opened and left so.

## Examples

- 彼は車を持っている。 He has a car. (Tatoeba 104634)
- 私は福井に行っています。 I am in Fukui (I have gone to Fukui). (Tatoeba 3673952)
- トムはまだ来ていません。 Tom hasn't come yet. (Tatoeba 9002411)
- まだ起きてる？ Are you still up? (Tatoeba 2569601)

## Notes

1. "Know" is 知っている, but "don't know" is 知らない, not 知っていない.
2. A few verbs appear only in this form and are learned as words, such as 似ている, resembles.
3. 行っている can also mean "goes regularly", as to a school: that is the later habit use.
4. Kindaichi Haruhiko's classification of 1950 calls verbs like 読む durative (継続動詞) and verbs like 壊れる instantaneous (瞬間動詞).
