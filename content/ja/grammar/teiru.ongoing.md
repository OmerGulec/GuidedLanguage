The action is under way at the time spoken of.

彼は本を読んでいる。 He is reading a book.

## How it is built

The て form of the verb, then いる: 読む → 読んで → 読んでいる. It conjugates as いる does: 読んでいない, is not reading; 読んでいた, was reading; 読んでいます, polite. In speech: 読んでる.

## Look-alikes

**The dictionary form.** 読む alone does not mean "is reading". For an action verb it means the act in general or one still to come: 毎日本を読む, (I) read every day; 明日読む, (I) will read it tomorrow. "Is reading" needs ている.

**A resulting state** (next). With verbs of a change that is over in a moment, ている gives the state after the change, not a change in progress: 壊れている is "is broken", not "is breaking".

**"Have been doing".** Where English says "have been waiting", Japanese still uses ている: ２時間待っている, (I) have been waiting for two hours.

## Examples

- 彼は今テレビを見ている。 He is watching television now. (Tatoeba 107196)
- 私はここで２時間待っている。 I have been waiting here for two hours. (Tatoeba 161064)
- 彼はその時本を読んでいた。 He was reading a book at the time. (Tatoeba 112729)
- 私は今本を読んでいます。 I am reading a book now. (Tatoeba 156905)

## Notes

1. On its own, いる means "to be there" and is said of people and animals. After a て form it is said of anything: 雨が降っている, it is raining.
2. School grammar calls this いる an auxiliary verb (補助動詞) and this use the progressive (進行).
3. A later use of the same form is a habit over a period: 毎朝走っている, (I) run every morning these days.
