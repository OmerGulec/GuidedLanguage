# 008 — Grammar without a single marker

Status: accepted

**Clauses that modify nouns** are one form (007), with uses split by the noun's role inside the clause: subject or object (昨日友達が買った本), another role such as place or reason (彼が育った町), or no missing role (魚を焼く匂い, 〜という噂). の as subject inside the clause (友達の買った本) is its own use. Card key point: the noun's role and where the clause starts.

**Length and nesting** rank passages by difficulty; they never add to coverage. One system lesson teaches how to unpack long modifiers and why to read to the end (〜わけではない reverses everything before it).

**Who does what.** Dropped subjects and carried topics are not items; their visible clues are, and those lessons say "this tells you who":

- connectives: ながら shares the main clause's subject; て, ので and たら clauses can have their own が but not は; から and けど clauses can have their own は;
- は reaches the main predicate; が stays inside its clause;
- direction (くれる, あげる), honorific and humble verbs, たい vs たがる, evidentials (そうだ, らしい), viewpoint (passive, てくる), speech style in dialogue, それ/そう/こう and 自分.

**Reader aids.** A toggle brackets modifiers and coordinations (AとBのC) and shows what they attach to. Tapping a predicate shows its dropped subject only where two models agree, marked as inferred, never inline. Taps are lookups (002).

**Passage questions.** Optional "who did X?" questions, made offline only where the answer is a noun phrase in the passage and two models agree, with a "question looks wrong" button. A miss is an event against the clue items involved, never a grade.

**Before building.** Noun-modifying clauses and dropped subjects join the 006 test set; aids and questions wait for their numbers. Whatever parses sentence structure uses the analyser's word boundaries.
