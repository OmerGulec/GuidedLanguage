# 014 — Placement

Status: accepted.

**Why.** Many learners arrive with vocabulary (an Anki deck, a word list, Renshuu) and no idea which grammar they know. Knowing a form's name says little about reading it right: someone who knows ている may still read 死んでいる as "is dying". What a learner can judge is whether they understood a sentence, so placement asks that.

**1. One question.** Where are you with grammar: starting from zero; finished a course; or read some Japanese without having studied grammar. Courses with fixed content (Genki I and II, Minna no Nihongo I and II) and imports (Bunpro, Renshuu) map to items through authored tables; Tae Kim and "studied for N5 or N4" map to stages, since their content is not fixed. The answer is a starting estimate, never a result. Starting from zero skips placement.

**2. A reading check,** at most 15 sentences up front, about five minutes.

- Each sentence is a library passage with at most one word the learner does not know, glossed where it appears. Its grammar is the tested use plus items already passed in this check or covered by the claimed course. Where the use has a look-alike, the sentence is one the look-alike would misread (死んでいる, not 読んでいる).
- The learner commits before seeing the answer: they choose between the use's reading and the look-alike's misreading, both written from the keys, or say "not sure", which counts as a fail (004).
- The check first samples every stage sparsely, then tests more densely where results are mixed. It always includes the frequent uses that have look-alikes (は as contrast, the uses of に and て, both uses of ている, の as nominaliser, the clauses of 008), since they weigh most in unknowns, and the structures a learner's first language may share (009). It skips what the starting estimate already settles. Items are chosen by library frequency times how unsure the estimate is. A stage is decided by two items or more, never one, and one failure never ends the check.
- "Finish later" turns the untested items into cards spread over the first week's sessions.

**3. The result as a list,** collapsed by stage, defaults set from the check: an untested item takes its stage's result (mostly passed: marked; mostly failed: taught; mixed: marked and reviewed early). Each item shows an example sentence rather than its name. The learner adjusts the list; ticked items become Marked known, with the source in the reason (placement, a course, an import), unticked ones are taught.

**4. Corrections afterwards.** A lookup or miss in the reader makes a grammar item due (013); every lesson has "I already know this". Placement only needs to be roughly right, because:

- First reviews of marks come in order of doubt: untested marks within about three weeks; marks passed in the check later.
- A failed first review of a mark withdraws it, so memory restarts from real reviews, and re-teaches the item; the item's marked dependents become due the next study day.
- How often each source's marks fail their first review is tracked; a course table's rows that fail often are revised. The same number measures placement as a whole.

**Scope.** Grammar uses and formations are marked known; systems are introduced (009). Words come from imports and "I already know this word". Placement covers reading; production rungs come with the goals that have them. An answer in the check on an item that already has reviews (an import that has faded) is recorded as a review of its sentence card, since it is the same card under the same rule.

**Sentences.** For forms with several uses, sentences come from the 006 test set, labelled by two models; translations from Tatoeba. Until both exist, step 3 stands alone, with the key's own example.

**Changes to earlier decisions.**

- 009's placement, a quick check for systems and structures, becomes this decision and covers all grammar.
- 013: a mark nobody has checked falls due within the doubtful period; a failed first review of any mark withdraws it and re-teaches the item, rather than counting as a lapse. Marks are ordered by doubt but not yet by frequency within it, and a withdrawal brings due the item's marked dependents but not its stage's other marks: core knows the graph, not the stages.
