# 010 — Lessons

Status: accepted

**Shape.** A form page holds the core idea every use builds on. A use page holds what it does, how it's built, the key (also the card grading rule), contrasts with look-alikes the learner knows or will meet soon, 3–5 library examples where everything else is known (more on request), footnotes (school-grammar names, parts, later uses) and further reading linking the sources compared.

**Length** follows the sources: every point made by at least two of the compared sources is covered; points made by only one become footnotes or are left out. Examples carry the weight.

**Process.**

1. Gather how at least three sources explain the item.
2. Write a comparison note: agreements, disagreements, what each does well. It is kept next to the lesson.
3. A model drafts the lesson in our own words. No copied text; examples only from our library.
4. A second model checks correctness against the sources. Disagreements stay flagged until someone qualified reviews them.
5. The owner checks clarity and approves.

**Files.** One file per item, next to its comparison note, with a header holding id, kind (form, use, pattern, formation or system), parts, prerequisites, nuance flag and key. The headers are the authored graph (009).
