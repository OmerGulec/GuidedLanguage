# 007 — Grammar structure

Status: accepted

**Forms and uses.** A form is an inflection (te-form), a particle (に) or a multi-part pattern that lists its parts (てもいい). A grammar item is one form with one use (006). Each form's page states its core idea; each use builds on it.

**Formation.** How a form is built is a skill, not a meaning. For reading, its cards run backwards (よんで → 読む or 呼ぶ?); building forms is drilled only for production goals.

**Explaining formation.** Kana rows: a godan verb's last kana moves along its row (か き く け こ) and each ending picks a row. Ichidan verbs drop る. て and た are one sound-change family. する and 来る are irregular. Forms are referred to by textbook labels (V-て, V-ない); school-grammar names (未然形 …) appear only as footnotes.

**Systems once, members as items.** A system is explained once, early and small: verb classes, kana rows and sound changes; the こそあど grid; counters; giving and receiving. Its members then arrive as ordinary items; endings arrive as "one more ending on a stem you know".

**Order.** A form comes after what it is derived from: plain before polite, だ before です. The rest of ordering is in 009.

**Patterns.** A pattern lists its parts as uses, with an author's note on how much they explain the meaning. When the last part is learned, the pattern gets a short "why it means this" step, keeps its review history, and its variants (なくてもいい) become cheap to learn. When a part is taught, its lesson shows the patterns the learner already knows that contain it. Authors can override this for parts never taught alone.

**Nuance.** A use can be flagged as nuance (sentence-final ね, politeness level). Nuance items never add to unknowns.

**Contractions.** ちゃう, なきゃ and similar are spelling variants of their full forms.

**Validation.** These choices were made on expertise, not by the owner. They are tested by building a small slice of the curriculum and having the owner learn from it; whatever doesn't work on a learner changes.
