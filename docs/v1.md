# v1 — the first version

Status: agreed with the owner on 1 November 2026, with the answers at the end.

**Purpose.** The owner studies and reads with it every day, on desktop and phone, in place of Renshuu. One goal, Reader. Accounts exist from the start (001), but there is no sign-up page: accounts are made with a command.

**Places** (005): Today · Library · Dictionary · You.

## Screens

**Sign in.** Email and password.

**Start.** Shown on first run and kept under You. Three steps, each can be skipped.

1. Import. Renshuu: paste the read-only API key; the server fetches the full history and replays it (013). Or a word list: paste `word reading` lines. Shows how many words matched, were guessed and were missing, and lists the guesses to confirm.
2. Grammar list (014 step 3, standing alone until the reading check exists). Items in stages, collapsed; each shows its key's example sentence rather than its name. A tick is Marked known, by hand. This replaces the checklist page; its 28 ticks are carried over.
3. Result: the share of the library the learner reads fully and with one unknown, and the first items Today will teach.

**Today.** The day's work as numbered steps with amounts and progress: 1. Reviews 84 · 2. New 8 (6 words, 2 grammar) · 3. Reading 3. One primary key, Continue, runs the next unfinished step; each step can also be started alone. At the end: done for today, coverage before and after, and Read more, which opens the Library.

**Review.** One card at a time: front, Show, back, then Pass or Fail. The progress bar is the title band. The last answer can be undone. Words in any sentence can be tapped, as in the Library. On a keyboard: one key shows, one passes, one fails. New items arrive here in the plan's order:

- a new word: a sentence where it is the only unknown, with its reading and meaning, then its card, repeated in the session until passed (013);
- a new grammar use or formation: its page, then its card;
- a system: its page; Continue introduces it;
- re-teaching: the item's page again, then its card.

**Library.** In v1, the reader. One passage at a time, the best next one (lowest unknowns, 009), with its source and a link to it. Readings over the text are a setting: off (the default, since tapping is how the app learns what was not known), over words not yet known, or over every word written in kanji. A tap is a lookup whether or not the reading was shown.

- Tapping a word shows its reading, meaning, status and a link to its page (a lookup, 013).
- Tapping grammar names the use and its key where the use is settled (013, Passages); otherwise it lists the possible uses the learner has been taught (006).
- Any tapped item has "I didn't get this" (a miss, 013).
- The translation stays hidden until asked for. Done records the passage as read and opens the next.

**Dictionary.** One search box: words by spelling, reading or an inflected form (the analyser already runs in the server, 011); grammar by name or written form. Two lists, Words and Grammar; Grammar is the curriculum in computed order, with the learner's status on every row.

- Word page: spellings and readings, most common first (004); meanings, the main one first; status and each card's mastery; 3–5 library sentences with translations; I already know this; Ignore.
- Grammar page: name and written form; key; the form page and the use's lesson where written (only ている so far), otherwise 3–5 library sentences where everything else is known, with translations, and the references under Further reading; the form's other uses already taught and how many are still to come (006); what it builds on and what builds on it; status, mastery, I already know this.

**You.** Coverage (fully known, one unknown) over time; reviews per day; settings: time zone, study-day start, new budget, grammar cap, review cap, passages per day, readings over the text; import (as in Start); export of all events as JSON; sign out.

## Cards

The Reader goal uses first rungs only (004). When in doubt, it's a fail. A marked item's first review is an ordinary card; failing it withdraws the mark (014).

| Card | Front | Back | Pass when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Word, `recognize` | The spelling alone. With a sentence, the word marked, when another word shares the spelling. | Reading, main meaning, other meanings smaller; a library sentence containing it where everything else is known, with translation. | Reading and main meaning both right. |
| Grammar use, `sentence` | A library sentence where everything else is known and the use is settled. The form is marked until the card graduates. | Translation, the use's name and key, link to its page. | The key point understood. |
| Formation, `undo` | A known verb in the form, from the library; a different verb each review. | The dictionary form and the rule from the key. | The exact dictionary form. |

## What the backend still needs

What is built so far, and what comes next, is in `docs/status.md`.


- **Pack.** English translations from Tatoeba: 232,797 of the 248,939 library sentences (93.5%) have one. Grammar names, written forms, keys, form pages and lessons from `content/`. Every sense of a word for its page; the first sense stays the grading rule.
- **Card passages,** built in core (013, Passages): a function that chooses the passage a card shows. It holds the item, has the fewest unknowns for the learner, was not shown for this item recently and has a translation. For a word's first meeting, the word is the only unknown. Reviews record the passage shown.
- **Settled uses.** 88 uses share a form with others. A card's sentence must have its use settled, and shares cannot do that: は is topic 90% everywhere. Rules settle 17 of those uses; the other 71, including every use of は, を, が, の, て and ている and most of に, have no sentence the rules settle. Their sentences come from the 006 test set, labelled in-session by two models as the use shares were, about 50 sentences per use, built in the order learners reach the uses. The same set later serves the placement check and the model pass, so v1 starts it rather than adding work.
- **Formation cards:** verbs in a form, taken from the library (a word followed directly by a grammar form the formation builds).
- **API and user database.** FastAPI; SQLite with accounts, sessions, settings and events; argon2 password hashes; events posted in batches with ids made by the client (013). The server keeps each learner's state in memory, so a request does not replay 27,000 reviews; when it re-derives is under the state cache below. It runs on the owner's computer and listens on the local network, so a phone on the same network can use it. Backups in v1 are the event export and a command that copies the user database.
- **Serving rules from 011:** the server refuses a pack in which an id referenced by an event does not resolve, and follows `id_map` when items are renamed; display policy is enforced in one serialiser with one test, so text licensed for analysis only never leaves the server.
- **Development.** A dev server that starts as any named learner, with time travel (012). Playwright screenshots of every screen for every named learner, for the owner to review.

**Web.** Svelte 5 in single-page mode, strict TypeScript, types generated from the API schema (003, 011). Tokens and primitives come first and screens use nothing else, since the look will change (status, Design). Type as recorded there: TeX Gyre Schola, a Mincho for Japanese (the system's, then Noto Serif JP), osifont only inside charts.

## Left out of v1, and how each arrives

Each of these arrives by adding data to the pack, a member to a closed union that screens already handle, a feature folder, or a named change to core (which needs the owner's approval). None needs a screen reworked.

Five shapes in v1 make that so:

- **A study session is a closed union of steps,** in the API (pydantic) and the web (TypeScript), each handled by an exhaustive `match` or `switch`. v1 has three members: a card, a page (new grammar, a system, re-teaching) and a word's first meeting. A card is told apart by ladder kind and rung, since rung names repeat (`cloze` is a word rung and a grammar rung). A new step or rung is a new member, and the type checkers list every place that must handle it.
- **The client sends its situation** (sound, keyboard) with every plan request, and the goal is a stored setting, fixed to Reader in v1. Core already plans by goal and situation for the four presets.
- **Events** are posted in batches with ids made by the client, carry their origin, schema version and sequence number, and are applied idempotently (013). Their instants come from the client, and the server rejects instants in the future. Stamped by the server on arrival, every event of a batch would share one instant and replay would scramble their order (013, amended).
- **The state cache** derives a learner's state on a miss and advances it only with new events that come after its last one and retract nothing. Anything else re-derives: an undo, an import with past instants, a change of time zone or study-day start. So late events, from imports, other apps or offline use, need no new design.
- **A passage comes with everything a tap shows** (each item's span, reading, meaning and status), so tapping needs no request.
